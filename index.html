Reply labelling for outbound experiments

Is that automatic reply really proof the email reached an inbox?

An out of office only fires if your email landed. A bounce, a delay warning or a gateway "verify you're human" notice fires before the inbox. Inbox Signal sorts every reply into one of those buckets, shows its evidence, and simulates what a labelling leak does to a link vs no link readout.

    All replies are synthetic, written for this demo. People, companies and domains are made up (reserved .example domain). Nothing here is RocketSDR data.

    What a labelling leak does to a "link vs no link" result

    RocketSDR's 7 Nov 2026 study used automatic replies as an inbox signal and found Microsoft-hosted prospects sent 0.32× as many when the first email had a link. That signal is only clean if bounces, delays and gateway notices are kept out of it. In the same post, 10,773 of 138,530 sends bounced (7.8%), while automatic replies were 0.19% to 1.13% of delivered emails. Notices outnumber real auto replies, so a small leak matters.

    Three buckets, twelve labels

    BucketLabelsInbox signal?
    Human replyinterested, referral, not now, not interested, unsubscribe, otherYes (depends on copy too)
    Mailbox auto replyout of office, left company, auto acknowledgementYes (ignores copy)
    Pre-inbox noticebounce, delayed, gateway hold or verificationNo

    How a reply is sorted

    1. Cut the quoted thread. Text under "On … wrote:" or an Outlook "From: / Sent:" block was written by the sender, often with "unsubscribe" or "out of office" in it.
    2. Machine notice? A delivery status report header (RFC 3464), a daemon or gateway sender, or an SMTP status code, plus hold, delay or failure wording.
    3. Auto reply vs person. Auto evidence (Auto-Submitted header from RFC 3834, auto subject lines in 5 languages, "no longer with", ticket wording, away wording) is weighed against signs a person answered the pitch ("can you", "send me", "follow up in Q2"). Close calls are flagged for review.
    4. Intent for human replies, in a fixed order: unsubscribe, referral, not now, not interested, interested, other.

    Honest limits

    • Rules, not a model. Fast and explainable, but they will miss phrasings nobody wrote down. A small model trained on real labelled replies should replace them, with these rules kept as a check.
    • Synthetic data only, same author for rules and test sets.
    • The simulator is a what-if tool. It shows the direction and rough size of a bias, not RocketSDR's actual error.
    • Whether gateway and delay notices reach the sender's reply inbox depends on the sending setup.

    Why this, for RocketSDR

    The links study leans on automatic replies because they "track whether the email arrived, not whether it persuaded". The planned seed box test will measure placement directly; until then, the auto reply bucket is the measurement, so keeping notices out of it is cheap insurance. The same labels feed reply intent and attribution.
